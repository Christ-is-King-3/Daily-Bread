Christian Art

From the life of Jesus Christ to the witness of His Church, these historic works invite us to look carefully, open our Bibles and consider what faithful discipleship requires. Art points us towards the subject; Scripture remains our authority.

Christ Healing the Blind

El Greco (Domenikos Theotokopoulos) · ca. 1570

El Greco brings the healing of a blind man into a crowded architectural setting. Watch the hands and the directions of the figures’ gazes: the scene gathers around an encounter of attention and trust.

The Christian inheritance

Beauty in the Service of Faith.

This collection brings together works from five museum collections, including the National Gallery of Art in Washington, the Walters Art Museum and the J. Paul Getty Museum, alongside The Metropolitan Museum of Art and Cleveland Museum of Art. It includes Ethiopian, Armenian and Byzantine Christian imagery as well as European works. It includes medieval manuscript leaves, Bible woodcuts, icons, stained glass and works on paper, alongside paintings from later centuries. Some images depict biblical events; others reflect devotional traditions or artistic interpretations.

  • J. Paul Getty Museum · 5 works
  • National Gallery of Art, Washington · 31 works
  • The Metropolitan Museum of Art · 17 works
  • Walters Art Museum · 51 works

Collection source and image rights are different. The museum name identifies where the work is held or the image was obtained. Public-domain or CC0 reuse is identified separately in each work’s credit. Each image is included under its museum’s public-domain or CC0 open-access terms. Copies are held with Daily Bread so that the gallery does not rely on external image servers. Artwork is shown in full; the enlarged view lets you explore the details. Museum records preserve titles, dates, attribution qualifications and provenance.

Daily Bread reflections invite visitors to examine the biblical message, pray and respond as disciples of Christ. Historical information remains distinct from our Christian commentary. Scripture links are provided where a direct connection has been identified, and art from different Christian traditions is shown without treating artistic tradition as equal to the authority of the Bible.
